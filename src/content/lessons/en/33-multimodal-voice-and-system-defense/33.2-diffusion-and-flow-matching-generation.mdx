Language models generate text one discrete token at a time from left to right. Images and video frames do not have a natural left-to-right order—a cloud at the top left must match the reflection in a lake at the bottom right, and consecutive frames in [lesson 10.1](/lessons/10-video-and-world-models/10.1-how-video-models-see-time/) must keep objects consistent across time. Rather than predicting pixels one by one, modern image and video generators sculpt the entire spatial or spatiotemporal canvas in parallel across a handful of iterative refinement passes.

This lesson explains the two mathematical engines behind that process—**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)** and **Flow Matching**—along with the latent compression and guidance levers that control production latency and GPU cost.

## Why generation happens in latent space

Running a neural network repeatedly over raw RGB pixels wastes almost all its compute on high-frequency texture imperceptible to human eyes. Take a modest $512 \times 512$ RGB image: it holds $512 \times 512 \times 3 = 786{,}432$ numbers.

Modern pipelines first pass training images through a pretrained **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**—an encoder-decoder pair trained to compress images spatially (commonly by 8x on height and 8x on width) into a compact **latent tensor** of shape $64 \times 64 \times 4 = 16{,}384$ numbers:

$$
\text{Compression ratio} = \frac{512 \times 512 \times 3}{64 \times 64 \times 4} = \frac{786{,}432}{16{,}384} = 48\times
$$

All iterative denoising runs inside that 48x-smaller latent space (typically on a **Diffusion Transformer**, or DiT, operating on latent patches, or spatiotemporal 3D patches for video). Only after the final step finishes does the VAE decoder run *once* to turn the clean latent back into visible pixels.

## DDPM: learning to reverse gradual noise

**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models** (Ho et al., 2020) frame image generation as reversing a corruption process. During training, take a real image latent $x_0$, sample standard Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, pick a random timestep $t$, and mix them according to a noise schedule $\bar{\alpha}_t$:

$$
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\, \epsilon
$$

At small $t$, $x_t$ looks almost like the clean image; at large $t$, it is indistinguishable from pure static. The neural network $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)$—conditioned on timestep $t$ and text prompt embedding $c$—learns one regression task: **predict the noise $\epsilon$ that was added**, minimizing mean-squared error $\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)\|^2$.

At inference time, start from pure Gaussian noise and subtract a small fraction of the predicted noise step by step. Original DDPM required up to 1,000 steps because its stochastic reverse paths curve sharply; later deterministic ODE samplers (like DDIM and DPM-Solver) cut that to 20–50 steps, yet the underlying noise schedule still bends trajectories near the start and end.

## Worked example: Flow Matching straightens the path

**Flow Matching** (Lipman et al., 2022) replaces DDPM's curved noise schedule with direct straight-line transport from a noise sample $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ at $t = 0$ to a data latent $x_1$ at $t = 1$.

Trace a concrete 1D example before looking at the general vector equation. Suppose our noise draw is $x_0 = -1.0$ and our target data value is $x_1 = 3.0$. At time $t = 0.25$ (one quarter of the way from noise to data), linear interpolation puts the point at:

$$
x_{0.25} = (1 - 0.25)(-1.0) + 0.25(3.0) = -0.75 + 0.75 = 0.0
$$

What speed and direction carry $x_0 = -1.0$ straight to $x_1 = 3.0$ in unit time $\Delta t = 1$? A constant velocity:

$$
v = x_1 - x_0 = 3.0 - (-1.0) = 4.0
$$

Now write the general **Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching** training pair for any latent tensor:

$$
x_t = (1 - t)\, x_0 + t\, x_1, \qquad v_{\text{target}} = x_1 - x_0
$$

The model $v_\theta(x_t, t, c)$ predicts the **velocity vector** $v_{\text{target}}$ at every point along the straight path. At inference, an ordinary Euler solver steps forward from pure noise $x_0$ with step size $\Delta t = 1/N$:

$$
x_{t + \Delta t} = x_t + \Delta t \cdot v_\theta(x_t, t, c)
$$

Because straighter velocity fields incur far less discretization error per step than curved diffusion trajectories, Flow Matching architectures (adopted across 2024–2026 frontier image and video DiT models) reach high visual fidelity in fewer sampling steps—and distill cleanly down to 4–8 steps for interactive products.

## Classifier-Free Guidance and the inference cost budget

A conditional generator trained solely on prompt-image pairs often plays it safe, producing bland average images that partially ignore rare details in the prompt. **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)** fixes this at inference time by running the denoiser *twice* per step: once with the user's text prompt ($v_{\text{cond}}$) and once with an empty prompt ($v_{\text{uncond}}$), then pushing the prediction further along the prompt direction by a guidance scale $w > 1$:

$$
v_{\text{cfg}} = v_{\text{uncond}} + w\,(v_{\text{cond}} - v_{\text{uncond}})
$$

Watch what that costs in production. Suppose one forward pass of your image DiT takes 35 ms on an H100 GPU:

- **30 steps with standard CFG ($2\times$ passes/step)**: $30 \times 2 \times 35\ \text{ms} = 2{,}100\ \text{ms}$ (plus ~80 ms VAE decode = ~2.18 s).
- **8 steps with a guidance-distilled Flow Matching checkpoint ($1\times$ pass/step)**: $8 \times 1 \times 35\ \text{ms} = 280\ \text{ms}$ (plus ~80 ms VAE decode = ~360 ms—a 6x speedup).

:::warning
Cranking CFG scale $w$ too high (for example $w = 15$ when the model expects $w = 5$–$7$) does not make the model "obey harder." It overshoots the learned data manifold, causing oversaturated colors, burnt contrast, and ringing artifacts around edges. Always sweep step count and $w$ together on a held-out prompt evaluation set.
:::

## Discrete text diffusion: parallel block denoising beyond pixels

Continuous Gaussian noise does not map directly onto discrete vocabulary IDs, so text diffusion adapts the corruption process into a **discrete state space** (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024). In **masked diffusion** (MDLM), the forward process replaces a growing fraction of tokens with a special `[MASK]` token rather than adding Gaussian noise. During reverse generation, the model starts from `[MASK]` positions and evaluates the categorical distribution across all masked positions in parallel; under the MDLM reverse kernel, each masked position is sampled independently according to the noise schedule—so a single pass can unmask zero, one, or multiple tokens in parallel rather than being structurally forced to emit strictly one left-to-right token per forward pass.

**Block diffusion** (Arriola et al., 2025) bridges full-sequence diffusion and autoregressive serving: it interpolates smoothly between autoregressive generation (block size 1) and sequence-wide discrete diffusion by denoising each fixed-size block bidirectionally while conditioning autoregressively on earlier blocks with a standard prefix KV cache. That hybrid structure restores KV-cache reuse and arbitrary-length generation while exposing a clean quality-versus-parallelism knob: smaller blocks stay closer to autoregressive perplexity, whereas larger blocks unlock more parallel token unmasking per step at the cost of higher sensitivity when strongly interdependent tokens are revealed simultaneously.

## Production tradeoffs for image versus video

Extending Flow Matching from images to video turns 2D spatial patches into 3D space-time patches (width × height × frames, as [lesson 10.1](/lessons/10-video-and-world-models/10.1-how-video-models-see-time/) introduced). Because self-attention scales quadratically with total patch count, a 5-second clip at 24 frames per second carries an order of magnitude more latent tokens than a still image—pushing generation from hundreds of milliseconds to tens of seconds and making asynchronous job queues, progress streaming, and watermark/provenance metadata mandatory in production APIs.

:::caveat
Fast 4-step distilled image generators cut GPU cost dramatically, but distillation narrows sample diversity and can lose fine typography or multi-object spatial accuracy compared with a 20–30 step teacher checkpoint. Route interactive previews to a 4-step distilled model and final print/marketing renders to the full-step checkpoint.
:::
