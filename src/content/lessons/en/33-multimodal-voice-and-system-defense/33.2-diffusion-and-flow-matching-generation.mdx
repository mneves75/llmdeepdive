Language models generate text one discrete token at a time from left to right. Images and video frames have no natural left-to-right order—a cloud at the top left must match its reflection in a lake at the bottom right, and consecutive frames in [lesson 10.1](/lessons/10-video-and-world-models/10.1-how-video-models-see-time/) must keep objects consistent across time. Rather than predicting one pixel after another, modern image and video generators refine the whole canvas in parallel across a small number of steps.

This lesson explains the two mathematical engines behind that process—**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)** and **Flow Matching**—plus the latent compression and guidance controls that set inference speed and graphics processing unit (GPU) cost in production.

## Why generation happens in latent space

Running a neural network repeatedly over raw **RGB pixels** (the Red, Green, and Blue color numbers stored for every tiny dot on a screen) wastes almost all its math on fine pixel grain human eyes barely notice. Even a modest $512 \times 512$ RGB image holds $512 \times 512 \times 3 = 786{,}432$ numbers.

Modern pipelines first pass training images through a pretrained **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**—an encoder-decoder pair that shrinks height and width (commonly by 8x each) into a compact **latent tensor** (a smaller grid of numbers keeping shapes and layout) of shape $64 \times 64 \times 4 = 16{,}384$:

$$
\text{Compression ratio} = \frac{512 \times 512 \times 3}{64 \times 64 \times 4} = \frac{786{,}432}{16{,}384} = 48\times
$$

Step 1 of the figure below traces this 48x-smaller pipeline: a **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**—the same attention architecture from Track 4 applied to small squares (patches) of the latent grid, or 3D space-time patches for video—runs all iterative denoising steps in latent space, and the VAE decoder runs *once* at the end to turn the clean latent back into visible pixels.

## DDPM: learning to reverse gradual noise

**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models** (Ho et al., 2020) frame generation as reversing a corruption process. During training, take a real image latent $x_0$, draw standard **Gaussian noise** (bell-curve random static, like TV snow) $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, pick a random step counter (timestep) $t$, and mix them according to a noise schedule $\bar{\alpha}_t$:

$$
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\, \epsilon
$$

At small $t$, $x_t$ looks almost like the clean image; at large $t$, it is indistinguishable from static. The neural network $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)$—guided by timestep $t$ and the text prompt vector $c$—learns a regression task (predicting continuous numbers): **predict the noise $\epsilon$ that was added**, minimizing mean-squared error $\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)\|^2$.

At inference time, the model starts from pure Gaussian noise and subtracts a fraction of the predicted noise step by step. Original DDPM needed up to 1,000 steps because its **stochastic** (random-step) reverse paths curve sharply. Deterministic **Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE)** samplers (smooth step-forward rules with no fresh randomness, such as DDIM and DPM-Solver) cut that to 20–50 steps, yet the underlying noise schedule still bends trajectories near the start and end.

## Worked example: Flow Matching straightens the path

**Flow Matching** (Lipman et al., 2022) replaces DDPM's curved noise schedule with straight-line transport from a noise draw $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ at $t = 0$ to a data latent $x_1$ at $t = 1$.

Trace a 1D example before the general equation (plotted in Step 2 of the figure below). Suppose our noise draw is $x_0 = -1.0$ and our target data value is $x_1 = 3.0$. At time $t = 0.25$ (one quarter of the way from noise to data), linear interpolation puts the point at:

$$
x_{0.25} = (1 - 0.25)(-1.0) + 0.25(3.0) = -0.75 + 0.75 = 0.0
$$

What speed and direction carry $x_0 = -1.0$ straight to $x_1 = 3.0$ in unit time $\Delta t = 1$? A constant velocity:

$$
v = x_1 - x_0 = 3.0 - (-1.0) = 4.0
$$

Now write the general **Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching** training pair for any latent tensor:

$$
x_t = (1 - t)\, x_0 + t\, x_1, \qquad v_{\text{target}} = x_1 - x_0
$$

The model $v_\theta(x_t, t, c)$ predicts the **velocity vector** $v_{\text{target}}$ along the straight path. At inference, an ordinary Euler solver steps forward from pure noise $x_0$ with step size $\Delta t = 1/N$:

$$
x_{t + \Delta t} = x_t + \Delta t \cdot v_\theta(x_t, t, c)
$$

Straighter velocity fields suffer far less **discretization error** (drift from approximating a curve with straight jumps) per step than curved diffusion trajectories. That lets Flow Matching architectures—used across 2024–2026 frontier image and video DiT models—reach sharp outputs in fewer steps and **distill** (train a faster copy to mimic a slower teacher) down to 4–8 steps for interactive products.

## Classifier-Free Guidance and the inference cost budget

A generator trained only on prompt-image pairs often plays it safe, producing bland average images that skip rare details in the prompt. **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)** sharpens prompt adherence by running the denoiser *twice* per step—once with the user's text prompt ($v_{\text{cond}}$) and once with an empty prompt ($v_{\text{uncond}}$)—and pushing further along the prompt direction by a guidance scale $w > 1$:

$$
v_{\text{cfg}} = v_{\text{uncond}} + w\,(v_{\text{cond}} - v_{\text{uncond}})
$$

Watch what that costs on an H100 GPU where one DiT forward pass takes 35 ms and the final VAE decode takes about 80 ms. Step 3 of the figure below plots both curves: 30 steps with standard CFG ($2\times$ passes/step) burn $30 \times 2 \times 35\ \text{ms} = 2{,}100\ \text{ms}$ plus 80 ms decode (~2.18 s), whereas 8 steps on a guidance-distilled Flow Matching **checkpoint** (saved model weights, $1\times$ pass/step) take $8 \times 1 \times 35\ \text{ms} = 280\ \text{ms}$ plus 80 ms decode (~360 ms—a 6x speedup).

:::warning
Cranking CFG scale $w$ too high (for example $w = 15$ when the model expects $w = 5$–$7$) does not make the model "obey harder." It overshoots the region of realistic images the model learned during training, causing oversaturated colors, harsh contrast, and ringing artifacts around edges. Always test step count and $w$ together on a held-out prompt evaluation set (a reserved test list not used in training).
:::

## Discrete text diffusion: parallel block denoising beyond pixels

Continuous Gaussian noise does not map directly onto discrete vocabulary IDs, so text diffusion moves corruption into a **discrete state space** (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024). In **masked diffusion** (MDLM), the forward process replaces a growing fraction of tokens with a `[MASK]` token instead of adding Gaussian noise. During reverse generation, the model starts from `[MASK]` positions and predicts a **categorical distribution** (a probability for every word in the vocabulary) across all masked slots in parallel; under the MDLM reverse kernel, each masked slot is sampled independently according to the noise schedule, so one pass can reveal zero, one, or multiple tokens at once rather than being forced to emit strictly one left-to-right token per pass.

**Block diffusion** (Arriola et al., 2025) bridges full-sequence diffusion and autoregressive serving: it interpolates smoothly between left-to-right generation (block size 1) and sequence-wide discrete diffusion by denoising each fixed-size block bidirectionally while conditioning left-to-right on earlier blocks with a standard prefix **KV cache** (saved attention keys and values from prior tokens). That hybrid restores KV-cache reuse and arbitrary-length generation while exposing one clear trade-off: smaller blocks stay closer to autoregressive **perplexity** (how surprised the model is by real text—lower is better), whereas larger blocks unmask more tokens in parallel at the risk of inconsistency when strongly linked tokens are revealed together.

## Production tradeoffs for image versus video

Extending Flow Matching from images to video turns 2D spatial patches into 3D space-time patches (width × height × frames, as [lesson 10.1](/lessons/10-video-and-world-models/10.1-how-video-models-see-time/) introduced). Because self-attention scales quadratically with total patch count, a 5-second clip at 24 frames per second carries an order of magnitude more latent tokens than a still image—pushing latency from hundreds of milliseconds to tens of seconds. Production video APIs therefore rely on **asynchronous job queues** (background waiting lines that return a ticket immediately and deliver the finished clip when ready), progress streaming, and watermark or **provenance metadata** (hidden tags recording how and when media was generated).

:::caveat
Fast 4-step distilled image generators cut GPU cost sharply, but distillation narrows sample variety and can lose fine typography or multi-object spatial accuracy compared with a 20–30 step teacher checkpoint. Route interactive previews to a 4-step distilled model and final print or marketing renders to the full-step checkpoint.
:::
