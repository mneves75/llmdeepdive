A well-crafted offline suite—combining retrieval metrics from [lesson 29.2](/lessons/29-llm-and-rag-evaluations/29.2-retrieval-and-generation-metrics/), calibrated binary judges from [lesson 29.3](/lessons/29-llm-and-rag-evaluations/29.3-llm-as-a-judge-and-calibration/), and private domain cases from [lesson 29.4](/lessons/29-llm-and-rag-evaluations/29.4-benchmarks-leaderboards-and-contamination/)—still decays the moment real users arrive. New product policies land in the index, seasonal queries shift wording, and upstream providers update model snapshots. Connecting offline CI replay from [lesson 23.5](/lessons/23-agent-evaluation-and-ci/23.5-ci-replay-and-live-trials/) with live production monitoring turns evaluation from a one-off launch checklist into a self-reinforcing *evaluation flywheel*.

## Three-tier budget: PR gate, release suite, and online sampling

Running every slow LLM judge on every git commit makes developers bypass CI; running zero checks on production lets silent regressions linger for weeks. Split evaluation into three explicit tiers with clear latency and dollar budgets:

| Tier | Trigger | Scope & checks | Typical cost & speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: PR commit gate** | Every pull request | 40 deterministic + fast golden cases (schema, citation IDs, Recall@5, abstention controls) | ~45 s, ~USD 0.15/PR; hard merge blocker |
| **Tier 2: Release / nightly suite** | Pre-release & nightly | 250 stratified cases × 3 trials, full retrieval metrics + calibrated judges ($\kappa \ge 0.70$) | ~8 min, ~USD 2.50/run; blocks rollout |
| **Tier 3: Online async monitor** | Live production traffic | 100% deterministic telemetry + 2% stratified async judge sampling | Async (0 ms user latency), ~USD 0.80/day |

Check the online cost arithmetic for a service handling 20,000 queries per day where one LLM-judge evaluation costs USD 0.002. Judging 100% of live traffic costs $20{,}000 \times 0.002 = 40\ \text{USD/day}$ (USD 1,200/month), mostly spent confirming that routine happy-path queries still pass.

Instead, log deterministic checks on 100% of requests (citation validity, latency, top reranker score, abstention rate) at near-zero cost, and run the calibrated judge asynchronously on a *stratified 2% sample* (400 traces/day): 200 uniformly random traces to track unbiased baseline quality, plus 200 risk-weighted traces drawn from user thumbs-down clicks, low reranker scores, multi-turn retries, and newly ingested documents. Daily judge spend drops 50-fold to:

$$
400 \times 0.002 = 0.80\ \text{USD/day}\ (24\ \text{USD/month}),
$$

while surfacing regressions in hours instead of weeks.

## Multi-trial reliability: pass@k versus pass^k

Language models are stochastic even at low temperature due to serving batching and floating-point nondeterminism. Running a 250-case release suite once can turn a flaky workflow green by luck. Anthropic's 2026 evaluation guide contrasts two multi-trial metrics over $k$ independent runs of the same case, where $p$ is the single-trial pass probability:

- **Capability (`pass@k`):** probability that *at least one* of $k$ trials succeeds:
  $$
  \text{pass@}k = 1 - (1 - p)^k.
  $$
- **Consistency (`pass^k`):** probability that *all* $k$ consecutive trials succeed:
  $$
  \text{pass}^k = p^k.
  $$

Plug in $p = 0.90$ and $k = 3$ trials:

$$
\text{pass@3} = 1 - (1 - 0.90)^3 = 1 - 0.001 = 0.999\ (99.9\%),
$$

$$
\text{pass}^3 = 0.90^3 = 0.729\ (72.9\%).
$$

Notice the gap: a 90% workflow looks almost flawless under `pass@3` (99.9%) yet fails at least once across three runs more than a quarter of the time (`pass^3 = 72.9%`). Use `pass@k` during early prototyping to check whether a model *can* solve a task at all; use `pass^k` (or tight confidence bounds on mean pass rate) in Tier-2 release gates when customers expect consistent answers on every single click.

## Closing the flywheel from live incidents to CI

Telemetry only improves quality when live findings flow systematically back into offline tests:

- **Capture & redact:** when online sampling or user feedback surfaces a new failure cluster, export the full trace (query, rewrite, chunks, prompt version, model version, reply) and strip any personal data before storing it.
- **Minimize & label:** assign the trace to its taxonomy bucket (or create a new bucket), confirm the ground-truth expected behavior with a domain reviewer, and add it to the versioned golden dataset alongside the fix.
- **Re-check judge calibration:** every month—or whenever online alert rates jump unexpectedly—pull 50 freshly sampled traces, label them with humans, and recompute Cohen's kappa (Cohen, 1960; Zheng et al., 2023) to verify the judge itself has not drifted.
- **Prune stale cases:** retire golden tests whose underlying policies were deleted or superseded so the offline suite stays fast, relevant, and trustworthy rather than turning into an unmaintained graveyard of false failures.

## Setting merge thresholds without alert fatigue

Avoid blocking every pull request on a 0.3% wiggle in a noisy LLM-judge metric. Enforce **hard zero-regression gates** on deterministic invariants (JSON schema validity, valid citation IDs, security/ACL filters, and explicit safety controls) and **paired statistical gates** on stochastic quality metrics (blocking only when a change drops Recall@5, NDCG@5, or calibrated faithfulness beyond a measured margin, or fails a paired check against the baseline commit). In track 32, these exact release gates tie directly into coupled prompt-model-index versioning and canary rollbacks.

:::note
Version your evaluation dataset, judge prompts, and retrieval fixtures in the same repository (or immutable artifact store) as your prompts and index configs. Comparing Tuesday's score against last month's score only holds meaning when both numbers were measured on the same frozen test split.
:::
