Every growing AI product hits the same architecture meeting: *Should we keep calling a direct model API, move to a managed cloud platform like AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex AI, or Azure AI Foundry, or rent GPUs and self-host our own serving stack?* Without numbers, that meeting turns into folklore—"self-hosting is always cheaper at scale" versus "never touch GPUs." Both slogans fail in practice because the right answer depends on **billing shape** (per-token meter versus fixed hourly capacity), **utilization**, and **operational ownership**.

This lesson builds directly on [lesson 8.11](/lessons/8-building-and-serving-stacks/8.11-serving-in-the-cloud/), [lesson 9.10](/lessons/9-hardware-and-infrastructure/9.10-cost-per-token/), and [lesson 31.4](/lessons/31-ai-system-design-and-caching/31.4-unit-economics-at-scale/) to price all three hosting models side by side.

## One workload priced three ways

Start with the concrete workload established in [lesson 31.1](/lessons/31-ai-system-design-and-caching/31.1-end-to-end-latency-budgets/): **1,000,000 monthly requests**, where semantic and exact caching absorb part of traffic so cached **pay-as-you-go (PAYG)** inference costs **USD 2,925/month**. Now place all three hosting models on the same table:

1. **Direct per-token API (PAYG)**: billed strictly per input and output token consumed. Total bill: **USD 2,925/month**. Zero idle spend on weekends, instant access to newest model snapshots, no GPU infrastructure to operate.
2. **Managed cloud platform with provisioned throughput**: platforms such as **AWS Bedrock** (Provisioned Throughput), **Google Vertex AI** (Provisioned Throughput), and **Microsoft Azure AI Foundry** (Provisioned Throughput Units, or PTUs)—all dated 2026 offerings—let you reserve dedicated tokens-per-minute capacity inside your cloud account. Using lesson 31.1's comparison, a steady reservation at **USD 2,600/month** saves USD 325/month (11.1%) over cached PAYG while shielding p95 latency from noisy neighbors.
3. **Self-hosted open-weights model on rented GPUs**: run vLLM on your own cluster. Production high availability requires at least two replicas across zones so one node failure or rolling upgrade causes zero downtime. Using lesson 9.10's baseline of USD 2.50/hour for an 80 GB-class GPU running 24/7 (**730 hours/month**):

$$
C_{\text{gpu}} = 2 \times 2.50 \times 730 = 3{,}650\ \text{USD/month (hardware rental alone)}
$$

Even before adding load balancers, container registries, egress, or engineer on-call rotation, a two-GPU redundant self-hosted cluster costs USD 3,650/month—more than both cached PAYG (USD 2,925) and managed provisioned throughput (USD 2,600).

## Comparing the three managed cloud platforms (2026 snapshot)

Managed cloud platforms sit between direct vendor APIs and self-hosting. They host closed and open-weights models inside your cloud provider's security perimeter and offer three billing modes: on-demand per-token PAYG, discounted offline batch inference (often ~50% lower with 24-hour turnaround), and fixed monthly/hourly provisioned reservations.

| Platform (2026) | Model catalog focus | Provisioned reservation unit | Primary enterprise draw |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AWS Bedrock** | Multi-vendor (Anthropic, Amazon Nova, Meta, Mistral, others) | Model Units (Provisioned Throughput) | Native AWS IAM, PrivateLink, CloudTrail, AWS EDP spend drawdown |
| **Google Vertex AI** | First-party Gemini + Model Garden partners (Anthropic, open weights) | Generative AI Throughput (GSUs) | Tight BigQuery/GKE integration, Private Service Connect, GCP commits |
| **Azure AI Foundry** | OpenAI family + partner & open-weights catalog | Provisioned Throughput Units (PTUs) | Microsoft Entra ID, Azure Private Link, enterprise MACC drawdown |

Notice why enterprises choose a managed cloud platform even when its on-demand per-token rate matches the direct API: **procurement and perimeter**. Calling a model inside your existing AWS, GCP, or Azure account reuses your signed Data Processing Agreement, draws down existing annual cloud commitments, authenticates via IAM instead of static API keys, and routes over private VPC links. The tradeoff is **feature lag**: direct model APIs often launch new snapshots, prompt-caching tweaks, or structured-output modes weeks before every cloud partner rolls them out in every region.

## Utilization and the real break-even formula

Fixed capacity—whether managed PTUs or self-hosted GPUs—wins only when your traffic keeps the reservation busy. Define the **effective cost per 1,000 requests** under a fixed monthly commitment $C_{\text{fixed}}$ (hardware plus engineering operations) serving $N_k$ thousand requests per month:

$$
\text{Cost}_{1\text{k}} = \frac{C_{\text{fixed}}}{N_k}
$$

And the **break-even volume** where fixed capacity beats a PAYG rate of $p_{\text{payg}}$ per 1,000 requests is:

$$
N_{k,\text{break-even}} = \frac{C_{\text{fixed}}}{p_{\text{payg}}}
$$

Plug real numbers into that ratio. Suppose your cached PAYG bill is USD 2,925 per 1,000,000 requests, and your redundant self-hosted cluster costs USD 3,650/month in GPUs plus USD 4,000/month in allocated platform engineering time (roughly 0.25 of a senior engineer's loaded cost for upgrades, autoscaling, CUDA/vLLM patching, and on-call), making $C_{\text{fixed}} = 7{,}650$ USD/month:

$$
N_{\text{break-even}} = \frac{7{,}650}{2{,}925} \times 1{,}000{,}000 \approx 2{,}615{,}000\ \text{requests/month}
$$

You need roughly 2.6 million monthly requests—more than double our baseline—just to break even on self-hosting, *assuming* an open-weights model matches your quality bar and your traffic is flat enough not to sit idle overnight. If daytime peak traffic is 5x nighttime traffic, you either buy 5x the GPUs (wrecking utilization) or build complex autoscaling and cold-start pools, or adopt a **hybrid base-plus-burst** pattern: cover steady base load with provisioned capacity and spill short spikes onto PAYG.

:::warning
Ignoring engineering operations cost is the single most common spreadsheet error in self-hosting pitches. A two-GPU cluster running vLLM still needs image builds, kernel updates, KV-cache tuning, health probes, failover drills, and 3 a.m. incident response. Always include an explicit monthly ops line item inside $C_{\text{fixed}}$ before comparing against managed endpoints.
:::

## Choosing by stage and constraint

Rather than guessing, map your workload against four clear criteria:

- **Choose direct PAYG APIs** when volume is under ~500,000 requests/month, traffic is highly spiky, or you need day-one access to the latest frontier snapshots with zero ops overhead.
- **Choose managed cloud platforms (Bedrock, Vertex AI, Azure AI Foundry)** when enterprise IAM, VPC networking, cloud-commit drawdown, or regional compliance matter—and add provisioned reservations only once steady base load exceeds ~70% of the reserved tier, with PAYG handling overflow.
- **Choose self-hosted GPUs (vLLM/SGLang)** only when sustained volume crosses your all-in $N_{k,\text{break-even}}$ *or* a hard technical requirement demands it: serving dozens of custom LoRA adapters, modifying decoding internals, running air-gapped on-premise, or guaranteeing zero third-party data transit.

:::caveat
Platform pricing, PTU minimums, and GPU hourly rates change every quarter. The 2026 platform names and the USD 2,925 vs USD 2,600 vs USD 3,650 figures here illustrate the billing geometry; always re-run $N_{k,\text{break-even}}$ with live rate cards and measured peak-to-average traffic ratios before signing an annual reservation.
:::
