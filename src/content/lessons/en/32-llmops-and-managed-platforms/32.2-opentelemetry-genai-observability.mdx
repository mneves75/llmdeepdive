When a traditional REST endpoint slows down, an application performance monitor points to a slow SQL query or a saturated thread pool. When an AI endpoint slows down or starts returning bad answers, standard HTTP metrics only tell you that `POST /ask` returned status 200 in 1.4 seconds. They hide whether the time went into vector search, a reranker, a retry after a rate limit, or a model that suddenly started writing three times as many tokens.

This lesson shows how to instrument an AI pipeline with **OpenTelemetry** (an open-source observability standard for traces, metrics, and logs) using its **GenAI Semantic Conventions** (the shared schema for LLM calls, tokens, tools, and agents) so every latency millisecond, dollar spent, and quality failure maps back to the exact `release_id` from [lesson 32.1](/lessons/32-llmops-and-managed-platforms/32.1-coupled-versioning-and-release-artifacts/).

## Reading a concrete RAG trace

A **distributed trace** records one end-to-end user request as a tree of timed operations called **spans**. Each span carries a start time, a duration, a parent ID, and structured key-value **attributes**.

Trace a single support question flowing through a RAG pipeline:

- `rag.request` (root span, `700 ms`, `release_id = "rel-2026-11-20-a4f2"`): the full user-visible turn.
- `retrieval.search` (child span, `45 ms`, `top_k = 20`, `index_revision = "kb-rev-42"`): embeds the query and searches the vector index.
- `retrieval.rerank` (child span, `35 ms`, `candidates = 20`, `kept = 5`): scores and trims chunks.
- `guardrail.input` (child span, `20 ms`, `verdict = "allow"`): checks policy rules.
- `chat claude-sonnet-4-5` (child span, `600 ms`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens = 1850`, `gen_ai.usage.output_tokens = 240`, `gen_ai.response.finish_reasons = ["stop"]`): runs inference, with `220 ms` to first token and `380 ms` decoding the remaining tokens.

Add the sequential child spans:

$$
T_{\text{total}} = 45 + 35 + 20 + 600 = 700\ \text{ms}
$$

Now ask a practical engineering question: should the team spend two weeks optimizing the vector database to cut `retrieval.search` in half? Looking at the span tree answers it in ten seconds. Halving 45 ms saves 22.5 ms—a 3.2% reduction on a 700 ms turn—whereas the 600 ms model span accounts for 85.7% of the wall-clock time. Without child spans, teams routinely spend weeks optimizing the 6% slice they know how to tune while ignoring the 86% slice.

## OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions

Early AI teams built custom logging wrappers or tied their code directly to one observability vendor's proprietary SDK. Switching backends later meant rewriting instrumentation across every service and agent loop.

The **OpenTelemetry GenAI Semantic Conventions** (published by the OpenTelemetry project in 2025) solve that portability problem by standardizing span names, attributes, and metrics across providers and frameworks. Your application emits **OTLP** (OpenTelemetry Protocol) data once, and any compliant collector can route it to Prometheus, Grafana Tempo, Jaeger, Honeycomb, Datadog, or an internal warehouse.

The core attributes worth requiring on every LLM span are small and concrete:

| Attribute | Example value | What it diagnoses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gen_ai.operation.name` | `chat`, `embeddings`, `execute_tool` | Separates generation from retrieval and tool execution |
| `gen_ai.system` | `anthropic`, `openai`, `aws.bedrock` | Attributes outages or rate limits to the exact provider |
| `gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model` | `model-2026-08-15` | Proves which snapshot actually served the request |
| `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` / `output_tokens` | `1850` / `240` | Drives per-feature cost attribution and verbosity alerts |
| `gen_ai.response.finish_reasons` | `["stop"]` or `["length"]` | Catches silent truncation and refusal spikes |
| `gen_ai.server.time_to_first_token` | `0.22` (seconds) | Splits prefill/queue delay from decode speed |

Pair those standard fields with two custom application attributes—`app.release_id` (from lesson 32.1) and `app.cache_hit` (`none`, `exact`, `prefix`, `semantic`)—and your dashboards can immediately slice cost, TTFT, and truncation rates by release version.

## Metrics, traces, and logs: what belongs where

Recording every token of every request forever is expensive and unnecessary. Observability works best as three complementary layers with different retention budgets:

- **Metrics (100% of requests, low cardinality)**: aggregated histograms and counters for latency (`gen_ai.client.operation.duration`), token totals (`gen_ai.client.token.usage`), error rates, and cache hit rates grouped by `release_id` and route. Never put `user_id` or `request_id` as a metric label, or cardinality explodes your time-series bill.
- **Structural traces (sampled or tail-sampled)**: full span trees with timings, token counts, finish reasons, and retrieval IDs. **Tail-based sampling** keeps 100% of slow traces (for example p95+), 100% of errors or `length` truncations, 100% of user thumbs-down feedback, and a small random sample (say 5%) of healthy traffic.
- **Content audit logs (opt-in, tightly governed)**: raw prompts, retrieved chunk texts, and completions, stored only where policy permits and separated from general engineering telemetry.

Check the sampling arithmetic on a service handling 1,000,000 requests per month. Suppose 2% are errors or slow outliers and you keep 5% of the remaining 98% healthy requests. Total stored traces are:

$$
N_{\text{traces}} = 1{,}000{,}000 \times (0.02 + 0.98 \times 0.05) = 69{,}000\ \text{traces/month}
$$

You retain every single failure and tail-latency outlier while cutting trace storage volume by 93.1% compared with logging everything.

:::warning
The OpenTelemetry GenAI specification explicitly marks prompt and completion bodies as opt-in because they frequently carry customer PII, credentials, or confidential documents. Never enable raw `gen_ai.content.prompt` or `gen_ai.content.completion` capture to a third-party SaaS telemetry vendor without redaction and legal sign-off. Default to structural metadata in OTel spans, and keep sensitive text inside a redacted, encrypted store with explicit retention TTLs.
:::

## Closing the loop to quality alerts

Latency and token counts catch operational bugs, but an AI system can still fail silently with fast spans and status 200. Attach asynchronous quality signals back onto the same `trace_id`:

- **Explicit user signals**: thumbs-up/down clicks, regeneration clicks, copy-to-clipboard events, or support escalations attached as span events on the original `trace_id`.
- **Online sample evaluators**: an asynchronous worker reads a 2% sample of traces (joined with redacted text inside your secure boundary), runs calibrated graders, and emits `eval.groundedness` and `eval.schema_valid` metrics labeled by `release_id`.

:::caveat
Do not run LLM-as-a-judge evaluators synchronously inside the user-facing request path. A synchronous judge adds 300–900 ms of latency and doubles token spend on every user turn. Emit the trace asynchronously, evaluate sampled traces out of band, and feed regressions into your alerting and rollout controller.
:::
