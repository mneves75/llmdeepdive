[Lesson 3.4](/lessons/3-sequence-models/3.4-seq2seq-encoder-decoder-bottleneck/) ended with a diagnosis in two parts. One fixed vector cannot hold everything a long source contains. And even if it could, it is computed before the decoder knows which word it is about to produce. Widening it addressed neither. The 2015 answer, from Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio, changes the interface instead of its size, and the change fits in one sentence: stop throwing the encoder states away.

## Keep every encoder state, and score each one

Let the encoder produce states $h_1,\ldots,h_S$, one per source position, and keep all of them. Before target step $t$, the decoder holds its previous state $s_{t-1}$, a summary of what it has written so far. A learned scorer compares that state with each source position and produces one number per position, called an **energy**:

$$
e_{ti}=v^\top\tanh(W_s s_{t-1}+W_h h_i+b).
$$

The matrices $W_s$ and $W_h$ project the decoder's current need and the source position's content into a shared space. The two projections are added, squashed by $\tanh$, and turned into a single score by the learned vector $v$. Nothing here is a similarity measure chosen in advance; the comparison itself is learned. A high energy means "this source position looks useful for the word I am about to write".

## From energies to weights: a worked example

Energies can be any size and any sign, so they are not yet usable as weights. Softmax fixes that: it exponentiates each score and divides by the total, so the results are positive and add up to one ([lesson 0.3](/lessons/0-orientation-and-foundations/0.3-probability-you-need/)).

Work an example by hand. Take a three-position source and suppose the scorer produces energies $e=(2.0,\,0.5,\,-1.0)$ at some decoder step. Exponentiate: $7.389$, $1.649$, $0.368$, summing to $9.406$. Divide: the weights are approximately $0.786$, $0.175$, and $0.039$. The step then reads a weighted blend of the stored states, $c_t\approx0.786\,h_1+0.175\,h_2+0.039\,h_3$. It is reading mostly from source position one, with a glance at position two.

Now change one energy: raise the second from $0.5$ to $2.5$, and the weights become roughly $0.371$, $0.611$, $0.018$. A difference of two in the exponent moved most of the read from one position to another. Softmax is sharply sensitive to energy gaps. That is what lets the mechanism behave like a lookup while remaining fully differentiable, so gradients can flow through it during training.

In general, a softmax across the source positions turns the energies into weights:

$$
\alpha_{ti}=\frac{\exp(e_{ti})}{\sum_{j=1}^{S}\exp(e_{tj})}.
$$

For a fixed target step the weights are non-negative and sum to one, and a mask drives padded source positions to effectively zero. The **context vector** is the weighted blend of what was stored:

$$
c_t=\sum_{i=1}^{S}\alpha_{ti}h_i.
$$

The decoder combines $c_t$ with its own state to predict the next token and update itself. At the next position it computes fresh energies and a fresh context.

## Why this removes both failures at once

Count what this bought. The capacity problem is gone, because nothing must be compressed into a single fixed vector and the summary is tailored to the current step. The credit-assignment problem is gone with it, which is the half people usually forget: source state $h_i$ now influences a late target loss through *one* weighted edge. Lesson 3.4 measured that path at roughly eighty-five recurrent transitions for a modest sentence pair, with a retention factor raised to the eighty-fifth power. Attention replaces that product with a single multiplication by $\alpha_{ti}$. Two distinct failures, one mechanism.

## The axis bug that a falling loss hides

This is the single most common attention bug ever written. You have a matrix of energies with target steps on one axis and source positions on the other, and you apply softmax to it. Along which axis?

Normalize across target steps by mistake, and the code runs, the shapes are identical, the loss decreases, and the model produces fluent output, because the decoder's own language modelling can carry a great deal on its own. What you have built is not attention. Each source position now spreads a fixed budget of one across the target steps, so a source word that matters to three output words has its influence diluted, and a target step's weights no longer sum to anything meaningful.

The correction is an assertion in code, not a code review. Check that every unmasked row over source positions sums to one within numerical tolerance, and that every padded position receives zero. Two lines catch an error that a decreasing loss curve will hide indefinitely.

## What the alignment map shows, and what it does not

The full matrix of $\alpha_{ti}$ values forms a soft alignment map: which source positions each target step read from. Plotting it was a genuine milestone. Translation produces roughly diagonal patterns, with jumps and spreads where phrases reorder, learned without any word-level alignment supervision. "Soft" means the model may blend positions, and gradients flow through every nonzero weight.

:::warning
The most common misconception is that these weights explain the model. A high weight shows that a stored value contributed strongly through one normalized routing operation, nothing more. Three things break the simple reading. Encoder states have already mixed context with their neighbours, so "position three" is not "the third word". Later decoder computation can amplify or cancel what was retrieved. And alternative weightings frequently produce near-identical outputs. Alignment plots are a good diagnostic and were decisive historical evidence. A causal claim needs an intervention (change the value, measure the output), not a heatmap.
:::

## From this mechanism to the attention in today's models

:::insight
Bahdanau attention changed the field's central question from "how can the encoder compress everything?" to "how can the decoder retrieve what it needs?", and that question is still the one being answered. The specific machinery moved on. Dot-product scoring replaced the learned additive scorer, recurrence disappeared entirely, and query, key, and value became three separate projections of the same information. But look at what runs inside Qwen3.8-27B's full-attention layers: 24 query heads reading over 4 shared key/value heads at a head dimension of 256, in 16 of the model's 64 layers (Qwen3.8-27B Model Card, 2026). The shape of the operation is exactly what this lesson derived: score a current need against every stored position, normalize, blend. Track 4 rebuilds it in that modern form. [Lesson 3.6](/lessons/3-sequence-models/3.6-linear-attention-fast-weights/) asks a different question about the same equations, one nobody thought to ask until 2020: what happens if you drop the softmax?
:::

One practical note before that. The design keeps the recurrent decoder, so generation is still sequential. Computing energies for every source position at every target step also adds real work, and that work grows with the product of the two lengths. The cost was worth paying to remove the bottleneck. It is the same cost that reappears, magnified, when attention is applied not between two sequences but within one, which is where track 4 begins.
