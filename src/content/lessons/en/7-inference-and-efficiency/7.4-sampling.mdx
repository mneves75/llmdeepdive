The Qwen3.8-27B model card ships two sampling presets and tells you to use them exactly: thinking mode at temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20; instruct mode at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.80, top-k 20. Two questions hide in those six numbers. What does each knob actually do? And why would a vendor recommend the "risky-sounding" temperature 1.0 precisely for its reasoning mode? Answering both is this lesson.

Start from what the model hands the sampler. After the final layer, the language-model head projects the last position's hidden state into one **logit** per vocabulary entry — 248,320 unrestricted scores. Softmax turns them into a distribution; **decoding** is the family of rules that then picks one token. Greedy decoding always takes the argmax: deterministic, cheap, and prone to repetitive, locally-safe text. Sampling from the untouched distribution preserves every tail token — including ones the model itself scored as implausible.

**Temperature** rescales logits before softmax. For logits $z_i$ and $T>0$,

$$
p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}.
$$

Watch it work on two candidates with logits 2.0 and 1.0. At $T=1$ the gap is $1.0$, so the leader's probability is $1/(1+e^{-1.0}) = 1/1.368 \approx 0.731$. At the instruct preset's $T=0.7$ the gap becomes $1.0/0.7 \approx 1.429$, giving $1/(1+e^{-1.429}) = 1/1.240 \approx 0.807$. Cooling from 1.0 to 0.7 moved the leader from 73 percent to 81 percent — a precise sharpening of confidence, nothing more. Below 1 sharpens toward greedy; above 1 flattens; exactly 1 leaves the model's distribution untouched.

**Top-k** keeps only the $k$ highest-probability tokens and renormalizes. Both Qwen presets set $k=20$: every draw happens among at most twenty survivors out of 248,320 — about 0.008 percent of the vocabulary. **Top-p** (nucleus sampling) adapts instead of fixing a count: keep the smallest prefix of the sorted tokens whose cumulative mass reaches $p$. A confident distribution may cross $p=0.95$ with two tokens; a flat one may need many (still capped at 20 here by top-k). **Min-p** is a third truncation style, keeping tokens with $p_i \geq \alpha\, p_{\max}$ — a cutoff relative to the leader rather than to cumulative mass; Qwen's presets do not use it, but you will meet it in local-inference runtimes.

Now the interesting question: why temperature 1.0 for thinking mode? The classic wrong turn runs the other way — "reasoning needs correctness, so set temperature to 0." Try that mentally on a long chain-of-thought. Greedy decoding collapses every step to the single most probable continuation; degeneration research (Holtzman et al.) documents how that yields repetitive loops and blandly safe text, and a wrong-but-confident step gets locked in with no mechanism to explore an alternative derivation. A reasoning trace is a *search process*: hundreds or thousands of tokens in which the model proposes an approach, checks it, sometimes says so and backtracks, and only then commits to an answer. Diversity across those branch points is fuel for the search, and mistakes made mid-trace are recoverable because later tokens condition on — and can correct — earlier ones. The final answer is the survivor of that process, not a single unrevised draw. Meanwhile the truncation stack is still on duty: temperature 1.0 preserves the model's honest distribution, but top-k 20 and top-p 0.95 fence off the tail where incoherence lives. Instruct mode inverts the trade: replies are short, every token is close to final output with no room for visible self-correction, so the preset sharpens ($T=0.7$) and tightens the nucleus ($p=0.80$) to favor the confident mode. The corrected intuition: temperature allocates exploration, truncation guards coherence, and the right split depends on whether the tokens being sampled are scratch work or the answer itself.

:::warning
Temperature is not a knowledge dial. If Qwen3.8-27B puts the correct token deep in the tail, raising temperature mostly elevates wrong tail tokens along with it, and truncation may delete it entirely; lowering temperature makes a confidently wrong mode more dominant. Decoding redistributes and selects among scores the model already produced — retrieval, tools, and better training fix what sampling cannot.
:::

Order of operations matters in practice. A runtime may apply temperature, top-k, top-p, penalties, and other processors in sequence before renormalizing, and identical labels do not guarantee identical outputs across libraries if the order, edge cases, or RNG differ. A seed reproduces output only when the whole execution path is deterministic. When you deploy the model through vLLM, SGLang, or Workers AI, pin the preset explicitly rather than trusting defaults — and record model version, tokenizer, and the full decoding configuration with every request you might need to replay.

The reliable mental sequence: the model outputs 248,320 logits; temperature reshapes them; truncation removes candidates; renormalization restores a distribution; a pseudorandom draw picks one token; the token joins the context; inference repeats. Separate the model's scoring from the decoder's choosing, and the two presets stop looking like folklore and start looking like two deliberate positions on one dial.
