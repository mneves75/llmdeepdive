Here is a question you cannot answer, and the fact that you cannot answer it is the lesson. What was Qwen3.8-27B trained on? The model card gives architecture, licence, sampling presets, and a benchmark table. It does not give a corpus, a token count, a language balance, or a cutoff date, and there is no accompanying technical report. Someone made every decision below for this model, and none of them were disclosed. That is now the norm for open-weight releases: you receive the weights and the shapes, not the pipeline that produced them.

So this lesson teaches the pipeline generically. Carry its shape away as a set of questions you know to ask of any release, including this one, where the honest answer to most of them is "not published." The pipeline has four stages: sourcing, filtering, deduplication, and decontamination.

## Where did each document come from?

**Sourcing** begins with provenance: a record of where each document came from and on what terms. Each document needs a stable identifier and metadata for source, acquisition date, language, licence or permission basis, and collection method.

Publicly reachable does not automatically mean legally or ethically suitable for every use. Robots policies (the `robots.txt` rules a site publishes for crawlers), contractual terms, personal data, copyrighted material, and removal requests all require explicit governance. A URL alone is weak provenance, because the content behind it changes.

## What does filtering remove?

**Filtering** removes material according to a declared policy, in layers:

- Mechanical checks catch empty files, parser failures, extreme repetition, binary garbage, and implausible character distributions.
- Language identification labels each document's language, which supports routing and control of the mixture.
- Quality classifiers can rank documents, but they inherit the values and biases of the examples they were trained on. A filter that prefers one prestige dialect erases useful regional language without anyone noticing.
- Safety filtering is more than one keyword list. Personal identifiers, secrets, malware, and exploitation material need layered controls, access restrictions, and protection for the humans who review the hard cases.

Record scores and thresholds so the choices can be audited later.

## Why remove duplicates, and across which sources?

**Deduplication** reduces repeated training examples. Exact hashes catch documents that are byte-identical after canonicalization (normalizing whitespace, encoding, and similar surface details). Near-duplicate methods catch copied pages with small edits: they compare shingles (overlapping runs of words), fingerprints, or locality-sensitive hashes, which are hashes designed so that similar inputs tend to collide. The unit matters: discarding an entire book because one standard licence paragraph repeats would be absurd.

Duplicates distort sampling weights, and under the next-token objective of [lesson 5.1](/lessons/5-pretraining-at-scale/5.1-pretraining-objectives/) that distortion is direct. A document mirrored a thousand times contributes a thousand times the gradient. That encourages memorization and shrinks the effective diversity of the corpus.

The classic wrong turn is deduplicating **within** each source and declaring the job done. Sources overlap: the same Wikipedia article reaches you through the encyclopedia dump, three mirror sites, two scraped forums that quote it, and a book that reproduces it. Per-source dedup finds none of these, because each copy is unique inside its own shard (one slice of the stored corpus). Cross-source deduplication over the merged candidate pool is what actually collapses the cluster of copies.

It must also run before the train/validation split, not after. Otherwise copies of one text land on both sides, and validation loss reports a number better than any genuine performance on unseen text.

:::warning
Never build the held-out set (the data kept out of training to measure the model) by randomly splitting an already-duplicated corpus. This is the most common way a training team lies to itself. The split is statistically impeccable and the leakage is total, because the randomness is applied to documents rather than to clusters of identical content. Split by deduplicated cluster, or by source and time, and verify with a near-duplicate scan across the boundary.
:::

## How do you keep test questions out of training?

**Decontamination** protects evaluation. It searches candidate training data for benchmark questions, answers, code tests, or close variants, and excludes or flags the overlaps. Exact matching misses paraphrases; fuzzy matching produces false positives on common phrases. So the threshold, normalization, benchmark version, and decision record are part of the reported result.

This is why the model card's numbers, SWE-bench Pro 61.7, GPQA Diamond 89.2, and the rest, are vendor-reported and await independent reproduction. Without a contamination report, a score is a claim, not a measurement.

## How often does the model see each source?

Mixture construction is where a pipeline becomes arithmetic. The key quantity is the **effective epoch**: how many times, on average, the run passes over a source's unique tokens.

Suppose (an illustrative assumption of ours, not a fact about any shipped model) you plan a 3-trillion-token budget and want a curated code domain to be 10% of it, but that domain holds only 50 billion unique tokens. You will draw

$$
0.10\times 3{,}000\ \text{B}=300\ \text{B tokens}, \qquad \frac{300\ \text{B}}{50\ \text{B}}=6\ \text{effective epochs}
$$

over that source. Meanwhile a 2-trillion-token web pool assigned 40% of the budget is drawn at $0.40\times 3{,}000 = 1{,}200$ B tokens, which is $1{,}200/2{,}000 = 0.6$ epochs: it is never seen once in full. Same corpus, same run: one source repeated six times and another sampled at 60% coverage.

Effective epochs per source, not the headline token total, is the number that predicts memorization risk. Recompute it whenever a mixture weight moves.

## What makes a pipeline auditable?

Version every artifact: raw snapshot, normalized text, filter configuration, duplicate clusters, decontamination report, mixture manifest, and tokenizer. Preserve counts before and after each stage without retaining unnecessary sensitive samples. A reproducible manifest should let another authorized operator reconstruct exactly which document IDs were eligible for a run.

:::caveat
Data quality is not a single number. Coverage, freshness, legality, language balance, safety, duplication, and evaluation independence conflict with each other, and a pipeline resolves those conflicts by making choices someone has to own. When those choices are undisclosed, as they are for this model, you can still evaluate the weights, but you cannot audit what they learned from.
:::

The model will learn the distribution you actually feed it, not the description in a launch post. And when there is no description at all, the only responsible position is to say so.
