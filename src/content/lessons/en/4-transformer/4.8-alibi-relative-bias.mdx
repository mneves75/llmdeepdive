## A third place for position: the score itself

There is a third place position can live. [Lesson 4.6](/lessons/4-transformer/4.6-positional-encoding-sinusoidal-learned/) put it in the input, added to embeddings. [Lesson 4.7](/lessons/4-transformer/4.7-rope/) put it in the score's geometry, rotated into queries and keys. This lesson's family puts it in the **score's value**: compute content compatibility first, then adjust the number directly according to how far apart the two positions are. Historically this road was seriously traveled (T5 and several prominent LMs shipped on it), and understanding it sharpens what the winning road actually won.

The concrete problem can be stated as a modeling **prior**, an assumption built into the model before it sees any data: most useful information for predicting a token sits nearby. Yet "nearby matters more" is nowhere in the machinery of [lessons 4.2–4.5](/lessons/4-transformer/4.2-self-attention/). Could we just tell the model?

Let $s_{ij}=q_i\cdot k_j/\sqrt{d_k}$ be the content score from query position $i$ to key position $j$ (the scaled dot product of [lesson 4.3](/lessons/4-transformer/4.3-scaled-dot-product-attention/)), and add a bias:

$$
\tilde{s}_{ij}=s_{ij}+b(i,j).
$$

Softmax then normalizes the adjusted scores. Routing probabilities change while value payloads stay untouched. The function $b$ is the design space.

## Learned buckets (T5) and fixed slopes (ALiBi)

**T5 learns $b$**, bucketed by offset. A bucket is a range of distances that shares one value: small offsets get individual buckets, large distances share coarse ones, and each head learns one value per bucket. That is a bounded parameter budget encoding the prior that the difference between 1 and 2 tokens matters more than between 1,001 and 1,002.

**ALiBi** (Attention with Linear Biases) strips even the learning away. In causal attention each head gets a fixed linear penalty into the past:

$$
b_h(i,j)=-m_h(i-j),\qquad j\le i,
$$

with slope $m_h$ varying across heads. Here $i-j$ is how many positions back the key sits, so the penalty grows by $m_h$ for every step into the past.

A worked example shows the texture. Take a head with slope $m_h = 0.5$ scoring three keys at distances 1, 4, and 8 whose content scores are all equal at 2.0.

- The biased logits (scores before softmax) are $2.0 - 0.5 \times 1 = 1.5$, then $2.0 - 0.5 \times 4 = 0$, then $2.0 - 0.5 \times 8 = -2.0$. That is a strong recency preference, though a distant key with much higher content score could still win.
- A sibling head with slope $m_h = 0.0625$ charges only $-0.0625$, $-0.25$, and $-0.5$ at those same distances. It is nearly indifferent, keeping long-range routes open.

Steep heads become local specialists, shallow heads long-haul channels, and content can always outvote the tariff. Nothing is a hard window; everything is a graded prior.

## Where the penalty goes, and what it does not guarantee

The classic implementation mistake is where to apply the penalty. The tempting wrong version reasons that attention weights are what we want to shrink, so it subtracts from the softmax *outputs*. That breaks normalization: rows no longer sum to one, and a subtraction of probabilities is not equivalent to any bias of logits.

The correction is the one already in the formula: the bias joins the *logits*, before softmax, exactly like the causal mask's $-\infty$ in [lesson 4.4](/lessons/4-transformer/4.4-causal-masking/). ALiBi is really a soft, sloped cousin of that mask.

:::warning
Extrapolation is where ALiBi is most often oversold. Its title, "train short, test long," reports an empirical result, not a mathematical guarantee. Being able to *compute* a bias at distance 100,000 (unlike a learned table, which has no row there) is necessary but nowhere near sufficient for long-context competence: attention patterns, data, and the evaluation task all decide. The same caution applied to sinusoidal formulas in lesson 4.6 and to RoPE beyond its native range in lesson 4.7; no positional scheme escapes it.
:::

## Why decoder LMs chose RoPE instead

Why, then, is this largely the road not taken for modern decoder LMs? The bias interface has genuine virtues:

- clean separation of "how compatible" from "how distant";
- visible locality in the attention matrix;
- near-zero parameter cost.

But it can express only what a scalar function of distance can express: a monotone (always falling, or always rising) or bucketed preference. RoPE encodes offset as *phase* across 32 frequency planes, which downstream projections can consume as rich, directional, content-interacting structure. It also composes naturally with caching and with tricks like partial rotation and frequency rescaling.

The field's revealed preference is visible in our course model: Qwen3.8-27B ships RoPE (in the partial, long-base, multimodal form of lesson 4.7), not ALiBi, not learned buckets.

## How hybrid layouts change the question

There is a deeper 2026 postscript, and it is the right closing thought for the whole positional trilogy. These three lessons implicitly assumed the architecture of 2017–2022: full attention at every layer. Under that assumption the positional scheme must serve every token pair at every depth, and long-context strain lands entirely on it.

Hybrid layouts break that assumption. In Qwen3.8-27B only 16 of 64 layers run full attention. The other 48 are Gated DeltaNet layers that process the sequence as an ordered recurrence: order is built into their execution, with no rotation, bias, or stamp required. The positional scheme now has to be excellent in a quarter of the depth rather than all of it, while the recurrent layers handle length cheaply. Debates that were fought as "which encoding extrapolates best" partly dissolve into "how much of the model needs pairwise attention at all," the question [lesson 4.15](/lessons/4-transformer/4.15-gated-deltanet/) takes up directly.

The durable picture for this family remains worth keeping: a content score plus a positional prior, position as the *price* of communication between two points rather than an address stamped on either. Even where ALiBi itself is not shipped, that framing (locality as a soft tariff on logits) recurs across sparse attention, sliding windows, and every scheme that must decide how much the past should cost.
