## What should a position signal do?

[Lesson 4.6](/lessons/4-transformer/4.6-positional-encoding-sinusoidal-learned/) ended with a complaint: absolute encodings stamp "this is token 37" onto the input, when what attention usually needs is "this key is three tokens behind this query." The concrete problem is to make the *score itself*, the query–key dot product, depend on relative offset. And it should do so without mixing position into the residual stream and every value vector along the way.

**Rotary position embedding** (RoPE) solves it with geometry: rotate queries and keys by position-dependent angles, and let the dot product do the subtraction.

## Rotating a 2-D vector, by hand

Start with the smallest possible case: two-dimensional vectors and a rotation of 30 degrees per position.

- Let the query and the key both have content $[1, 0]$, an arrow pointing right.
- Put the key at position 1 and the query at position 3, two tokens later. The key turns by 30 degrees and the query by 90 degrees. Their dot product is the cosine of the 60-degree gap between them: $0.5$.
- Now move both four positions later, to 5 and 7. The key turns by 150 degrees and the query by 210 degrees. The gap is still 60 degrees, and the dot product is still $0.5$.

Each vector was turned by its own absolute position, yet the score only saw the distance between them. That is the whole trick; the rest is making it precise and scaling it up.

## Why the dot product sees only the offset

In general, a feature pair $(x_0,x_1)$ at position $m$ is rotated by angle $m\theta$:

$$
R(m\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(m\theta)&-\sin(m\theta)\\
\sin(m\theta)&\cos(m\theta)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The content projection supplies the arrow; position turns it. Rotation preserves the vector's norm (its length), so the score statistics of [lesson 4.3](/lessons/4-transformer/4.3-scaled-dot-product-attention/) survive intact. The payoff is the identity

$$
(R(m\theta)q)^\top(R(n\theta)k)
=q^\top R((n-m)\theta)k.
$$

In words: two absolute rotations meet in a dot product and collapse into one rotation by the *difference* $n-m$. Each vector is rotated by its own absolute index, yet the score depends only on content and relative displacement, exactly the interface we asked for. Values are not rotated, nothing is added to embeddings, and the mechanism composes cleanly with the causal mask and with caching.

The full scheme repeats this trick across many feature pairs, each pair a separate rotation plane, at geometrically spaced frequencies $\theta_i$ derived from a base $\theta$. Fast planes resolve fine offsets; slow planes cover broad ranges. It is the sinusoidal clock-bank idea from lesson 4.6, relocated from the input to the score.

:::warning
"RoPE is a positional embedding, so it's added to the token embeddings" is a natural reading of the name, and it is wrong. Nothing is added anywhere. RoPE is an operation applied to queries and keys after their projections and before their dot product; RoPE adds nothing to the residual stream and never rotates the values, although both can already carry position-dependent information computed by earlier layers. Confusing the two matters in practice: a cached key already carries its rotation, and code that rotates it again, or restarts position indices wrongly, compares the wrong offsets without raising any error.
:::

## How Qwen3.8-27B configures RoPE

**Partial rotation.** A reasonable but wrong assumption is that a model rotates *all* of each head's dimensions. Qwen3.8-27B does not. Its configuration sets `partial_rotary_factor` to 0.25, and the arithmetic is worth doing by hand. Head dimension is 256, so the rotated slice is

$$
256 \times 0.25 = 64
$$

dimensions per head. Since each rotation plane consumes a pair of dimensions, that is $64 \div 2 = 32$ planes. The remaining $256 - 64 = 192$ dimensions of every query and key are not rotated directly by RoPE.

That does **not** make them position-free: their hidden-state inputs may already encode position through earlier attention or recurrence. Partial rotation concentrates the direct rotary phase signal (the angle position adds) into a dedicated subspace, while leaving most dimensions available for unrotated matching.

**The frequency base.** Classic RoPE used $\theta = 10{,}000$; Qwen3.8-27B uses $\theta = 10{,}000{,}000$. With the usual frequencies $\theta_i=\theta^{-2i/d}$, a larger base stretches the wavelength (the number of positions a plane needs for one full turn) of every plane except the first: plane $i=0$ has exponent zero and turns one radian per position whatever the base. The other planes slow down. Positions hundreds of thousands of tokens apart then still land at distinguishable phases, a prerequisite for its native context of 262,144 tokens.

**Multimodal sections.** The 32 planes are not one undifferentiated bank. The model is multimodal, and a video patch (a small square cut from a frame) has *three* meaningful coordinates: when (frame), and where (row, column). Qwen's **MRoPE** (multimodal RoPE) splits the 32 planes into interleaved sections of $[11, 11, 10]$ (check: $11 + 11 + 10 = 32$), assigned to temporal, height, and width positions respectively.

For ordinary text the three coordinates coincide with the token index. For images and video, each section rotates by its own axis, giving the same attention mechanism a native notion of two-dimensional and temporal layout. How image patches enter the token sequence at all is [lesson 4.17](/lessons/4-transformer/4.17-multimodal-vision-encoder/)'s story.

## Beyond 262,144 tokens, and during decode

Beyond the native 262,144 tokens, the model card specifies extension to 1M tokens via YaRN, a method that rescales the frequency schedule so unfamiliar positions land in familiar phase ranges rather than extrapolating raw angles. The caution from lesson 4.6 applies with full force. A formula that *produces* angles at position 900,000 does not promise the trained network behaves there. That is why extension methods exist, and why they are evaluated on long-range retrieval rather than assumed.

RoPE's interaction with decode deserves a preview. Cached keys keep the rotation of their original absolute position, while each new query arrives with the current position's rotation. So the relative-offset identity keeps holding as the sequence grows, one of several reasons this scheme won in decoder-only models. The cache arithmetic itself lands in [lesson 7.2](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.2-the-kv-cache/). Only Qwen's 16 full-attention layers do any of this; its 48 DeltaNet layers track order recurrently, with no rotary machinery at all ([lesson 4.15](/lessons/4-transformer/4.15-gated-deltanet/)).

The lasting insight: position can enter a model through an *operation* rather than an *addition*. RoPE turns displacement into relative phase inside query–key similarity. Qwen3.8-27B shows the 2026 refinements: rotate only a quarter of the head, slow the clock by three orders of magnitude, and deal the planes out across time, height, and width.
