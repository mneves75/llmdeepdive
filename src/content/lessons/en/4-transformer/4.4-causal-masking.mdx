## Why would an unmasked model cheat?

Here is the trap every next-token trainer must avoid. Training data arrives as complete sequences: the model sees "The cache stores keys" all at once, yet it is being graded on predicting each token from only the ones before it. Self-attention, as built in [lesson 4.2](/lessons/4-transformer/4.2-self-attention/) and [lesson 4.3](/lessons/4-transformer/4.3-scaled-dot-product-attention/), lets every position read every other position.

Left unconstrained, the position predicting "stores" would just attend to "stores" sitting right there in the tensor and copy it forward. It would achieve a spectacular training loss (the error score training minimizes, [lesson 2.4](/lessons/2-neural-network-fundamentals/2.4-loss-functions-cross-entropy/)) that means nothing. The model would learn to cheat, and generation, where the future genuinely does not exist yet, would collapse.

Formally, a causal language model learns the factorization

$$
p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(x_i\mid x_{<i}),
$$

which says: the probability of a whole sequence is the product of each token's probability given only the tokens before it. So the computation for position $i$ may depend on earlier tokens only. **Causal masking** makes attention obey that factorization while keeping training parallel.

## The triangle on a four-token example

<Figure id="causal-mask" />

Work it through on a real prompt. Take the four-word input "The cache stores keys" and, for this example, treat each word as one token. A real tokenizer, as [lesson 1.2](/lessons/1-text-to-tensors/1.2-tokenization-i/) showed, may split words further: Qwen3.8-27B's tokenizer maps 248,077 ids, while its output tensor has 248,320 rows; the mask applies to token positions whatever they turn out to be. Number the positions 1–4. The allowed-attention pattern is lower triangular, with 1 meaning "may read" and 0 meaning "may not":

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
1&1&0&0\\
1&1&1&0\\
1&1&1&1
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Row $i$ is the reading query position; column $j$ is the key being read.

- Position 1, "The," sees only itself.
- Position 2, "cache," sees "The" and itself.
- Position 3, "stores," sees "The cache stores", and above all not "keys," the very token its hidden state is about to predict.
- Position 4 sees everything.

Implementations realize this with an **additive mask**: 0 on allowed entries, $-\infty$ on forbidden ones, added to the scaled scores $QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$ before softmax. Those scaled scores are the **logits**, the raw numbers softmax turns into weights. Since $\exp(-\infty)=0$, forbidden positions get exactly zero weight. It is the same exponentiation from lesson 4.3, used here as an off switch.

Floating-point formats do have a value for negative infinity, and many implementations add exactly that. Others use a very negative finite value, or a kernel flag that tells the attention routine to skip forbidden entries altogether. A casually chosen "large negative" constant can be insufficient in low-precision dtypes (number formats such as float16, whose range of representable values is far narrower than float32's).

## Why masking the loss is not enough

Now the classic wrong fix, because it is genuinely tempting. "Why mask attention at all? Just compute the loss only from each position's own prediction. Future tokens have their own loss terms, so nothing is stolen."

That masks the *grading*, not the *information*. Even with a perfectly per-position loss, unmasked attention lets position 3's hidden state absorb features of "keys" from position 4's representation at an earlier layer, or read position 4 directly. The leak is in the activations (the intermediate values the network computes), not the loss. The correction is to constrain the computation graph itself, which is exactly what the triangular mask does.

:::warning
"The loss already handles causality" sounds right and is wrong. The training tensor may contain the future, but the computation graph for each prediction must not, and only an attention-level mask (not a loss-level one) enforces that. A related off-by-one lurks in target alignment: inputs `[BOS, cats, sleep]` pair with targets `[cats, sleep, EOS]`, where BOS and EOS are special begin- and end-of-sequence tokens. A position may attend to *itself* because its state predicts the *next* token, not its own input.
:::

## What the triangle buys: parallel training and fast decode

The mask gives you two things that usually conflict: sequential *information flow* with parallel *training computation*. All rows of the score matrix are computed in one batched operation, yet row 3 depends only on columns 1–3. An RNN enforces causality by executing in order; a Transformer enforces it by connectivity, and so trains every position of every sequence simultaneously.

The same triangle explains **decode**, the phase where the model generates text. During generation the model holds a prefix, produces one token, appends it, and repeats. Because the mask guarantees that position $j$'s key and value are read only by positions $\geq j$, and never revised by later ones, the per-position K and V computed while processing the prefix are final. They can be stored and reused instead of recomputed at every step. That storage is the KV cache; its full arithmetic for Qwen3.8-27B lands in [lesson 7.2](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.2-the-kv-cache/).

In Qwen the mask story has one twist worth previewing. Only its 16 full-attention layers implement causality with this triangular mask. The other 48 Gated DeltaNet layers are causal by construction, processing tokens as an ordered recurrence ([lesson 4.15](/lessons/4-transformer/4.15-gated-deltanet/)).

## What the causal mask is not

Keep the causal mask distinct from its neighbors:

- A **padding mask** hides placeholder positions when different-length sequences share a batch (a group of sequences processed together). Those positions are invalid by length, not by order.
- **Packed-document training** may add a boundary mask so one document cannot attend into the previous one.

A batch can need all of these at once.

Causality is also not the same as knowing *positions*. The triangle says which tokens are earlier, but distinguishing "dog bites man" from "man bites dog" among the visible tokens requires the positional information of [lessons 4.6–4.8](/lessons/4-transformer/4.6-positional-encoding-sinusoidal-learned/). Bidirectional encoders like BERT drop the triangle entirely and pay for it by being unable to generate left-to-right.

The durable rule fits in one sentence: the training tensor may contain the future, but no prediction's computation may. A triangular matrix turns that rule into structure. It preserves parallel training, protects the objective, and makes fast decode possible as a side effect.
