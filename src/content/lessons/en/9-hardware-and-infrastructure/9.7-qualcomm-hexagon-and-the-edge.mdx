A product manager asks the only question that matters for a mobile roadmap: can we ship Qwen3.8-27B in the app, so it runs on the phone with no network round trip? This lesson answers no. That answer is worth a whole lesson, because reaching a well-supported no is an engineering result. Most edge disappointments ("edge" meaning on the user's own device rather than in a data center) come from teams who never did this arithmetic and found the ceiling three months into an integration.

## How much memory does one app really get?

Start with the memory budget, which is not the number on the spec sheet. A current flagship handset ships with something in the region of 12 to 16 GB of DRAM (its main memory), but an application does not own that machine. The operating system, the compositor (the part that draws the screen), the camera stack and every background app share it. Under pressure, the OS also reclaims memory from foreground applications.

A realistic sustained budget for one application's model weights is a few gigabytes. Take 3 GB as a stated working assumption for the rest of this lesson, and adjust it for your own platform. Now bring the quantization ladder from [lesson 7.9](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.9-quantization-i-int8-int4-the-basics/) (the same weights stored at 16, 8 or 4 bits each) and the artifact sizes from [lesson 7.12](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.12-running-models-locally/):

$$
17.1\ \text{GB (4-bit artifact)} \div 3\ \text{GB (budget)} \approx 5.7\times\ \text{too large}.
$$

## Why quantizing harder does not close the gap

The usual wrong turn arrives immediately: quantize harder. Two bits instead of four should halve the size and bring it into range. Do the arithmetic instead of hoping. Two bits is a quarter of a byte, so:

$$
27\text{e}9 \times 0.25\ \text{B} = 6.75\ \text{GB},
$$

and that is before the group scales and mixed-precision tensors that [lesson 7.10](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.10-quantization-ii-gptq-awq-gguf-qat/) showed always come along. So it is still more than twice the budget. The move fails on size, and it fails harder on quality: lesson 7.9's degradation curve is gentle from 16 to 8 to 4 bits and steep below that.

Push the logic to its end and it becomes absurd. Fitting 27 billion parameters into 3 GB requires

$$
\frac{3\ \text{GB} \times 8\ \text{bits/B}}{27\text{e}9\ \text{params}} \approx 0.89\ \text{bits per weight},
$$

under one bit per parameter. That is not a quantization setting; it is a research programme.

## What fits: solve for the model instead

Solve the equation the other way and it becomes a design brief. At 4 bits, a 3 GB weight budget affords about 6 billion parameters, and that is before the KV cache, the runtime workspace and the tokenizer.

Do not reuse [lesson 7.2](/lessons/7-inference-and-efficiency/7.2-the-kv-cache/)'s 64 KiB per token here: that value belongs to Qwen3.8-27B's 16 caching layers. For the 3-to-4-billion-parameter model you choose, derive the KV bytes per token as layers × KV heads × head dimension × two tensors (keys and values) × bytes per element, then multiply by 8,192 tokens.

A sober target is therefore a model in the 3-to-4-billion-parameter range. That is precisely what [lesson 6.11](/lessons/6-post-training-and-alignment/6.11-distillation-making-small-models-punch-up/) builds: a **distilled sibling**, a genuinely smaller network trained against the large model's outputs, inheriting behaviour rather than weights. The two techniques shrink different terms:

- Quantization reduces bytes per parameter, and it bottoms out.
- Distillation reduces the parameter count, the only term that gets you through the door.

## Bandwidth gives the same answer

Bandwidth confirms the verdict independently, and two unrelated arguments agreeing is what makes a negative result reassuring. Mobile LPDDR (the low-power memory used in phones) has bandwidth an order of magnitude below desktop unified memory and two below data-center HBM. Take 60 GB/s as a stated order-of-magnitude assumption, not a vendor quote.

Using artifact size only as a proxy for bytes read per token, [lesson 9.1](/lessons/9-hardware-and-infrastructure/9.1-memory-bandwidth-vs-flops/)'s division gives about 3.5 tokens per second for the pinned 17.1 GB artifact, and roughly 30 for a 2 GB one. Even in a fantasy where the large model fitted, it would be unusable.

## What the Hexagon NPU runs well

So what does the silicon actually offer? Qualcomm's Hexagon NPU (neural processing unit) is an accelerator leaning toward fixed functions, tuned for quantized integer arithmetic at low power. Qualcomm's developer documentation exposes it through AI Engine Direct, the QNN stack. AI Hub is the compile-and-validate path that targets a specific device family.

The mental model that matters: the NPU is fast and efficient only for graphs it fully supports. NPUs prefer static shapes (tensor sizes fixed in advance), quantized weights and a fixed set of operators. Anything outside that set falls back to the CPU or GPU. A graph that falls back on a hot path (code that runs on every token) has lost its power advantage and still carries its integration cost.

Qwen3.8-27B is a hybrid architecture whose Gated DeltaNet layers are new. Vendor kernel coverage for brand-new operators arrives in specific toolchain releases, exactly as lesson 7.12 warned about local runtimes. Verify operator coverage on your target device before committing to an architecture, not after.

:::warning
The most expensive misconception in edge AI is that on-device is a deployment decision. It is an architecture decision made at training time. You do not take a frontier model to the edge; you train a model for the edge and evaluate it on your own task. A distilled 3-billion-parameter sibling is a different model with different failure modes, not a lossy copy of the 27B. Benchmark it against your actual workload before promising parity, and remember that the vendor-reported benchmark scores on the large model's card say nothing about the small one.
:::

:::caveat
Every hardware figure here is either a vendor-published specification or, where marked, an order-of-magnitude assumption stated so you can substitute your own. NPU throughput claims in particular are usually quoted as peak integer operations under ideal conditions, on a specific quantized graph. They are not a token-rate prediction for your model. Measure on the device, with the toolchain version pinned.
:::

The lasting lesson is the shape of the answer. Sizing produced a clean, defensible no, and the no pointed directly at the right yes: **hybrid deployment**. A small distilled model handles latency-sensitive and offline work on the Hexagon NPU, and the 27B is called over the network when the task genuinely needs it. That split is a cost and reliability decision as much as a technical one, and [lesson 9.10](/lessons/9-hardware-and-infrastructure/9.10-cost-per-token/) gives it a price.
