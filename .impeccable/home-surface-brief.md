Scope: bilingual homepage and its shared entry experience. Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience: independent learners deciding whether this course can take them from
first principles to real model systems without hype or hand-waving.

Job and action: understand the course's difference within one viewport, then
start the first lesson, inspect the transformer anatomy, or choose a track.

Proof on hand: 158 bilingual lessons; eight finished tracks; cited claims; the
concept → analogy → lab → teach-back → quiz method; the real transformer explorer;
free/open-source/account-free/tracking-free product constraints.

Constraints: preserve real copy and claims, static anonymous HTML, EN/pt-BR
parity, semantic navigation, useful content before WebGL, and existing bundle and
performance budgets.

Chosen direction: Abyssal Core Atlas. The first viewport must turn curriculum
depth and transformer structure into one legible cross-section. The memorable
moment is following a signal from surface intuition through the model layers and
seeing the eight tracks continue that descent.

Chosen composition: Diagonal Descent. Its continuous curriculum spine carries
the cross-section, value proposition, and eight-track rail across desktop and
mobile. Core UI remains semantic code rather than rasterized mockup content.
